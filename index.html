Mentoring Young Minds

Community Connect @ SRMIST

As part of SRM Institute of Science and Technology’s Community Connect course, I had the privilege of volunteering with the NGO United Forum for Health & Environment, which provides free education to students from Class 1 to 8 in underserved communities.

Over the course of 1.5 months, I didn't just teach computer fundamentals or programming — I built relationships, shared laughter, and witnessed curiosity bloom into confidence. From logic-based puzzles to hands-on Python lessons, and finally guiding them as they launched their very first website, every day was filled with purpose and pride.

Seeing the students bring their ideas to life and host a website on their own was one of the most fulfilling moments of my life. The live site, a reflection of their imagination and grit, can be explored here: studentsofuhffhe.vercel.app.

Even after the program ended, I couldn’t walk away. I continue to mentor them during hometown visits and actively work to share their creations with a wider audience. This platform has become more than just a website — it's a voice for their potential, a call to action for parents and funders alike.

Watching these young minds grow despite all odds has changed me. Their resilience, respect, and hunger to learn taught me more than I could ever teach them. Proud is truly an understatement.

Looking ahead, I aspire to scale this initiative by collaborating with more NGOs and educational institutions, bringing technology-driven learning to every corner where opportunity is scarce. This experience has reaffirmed my belief that with the right guidance and tools, even the smallest spark of curiosity can ignite lifelong change. I am committed to nurturing that spark — one child, one class, one community at a time.
